Privacy, in plain English.

This early-access service stores account and monitoring data in the operator's Neon PostgreSQL database. The operator must replace this draft with a policy reviewed for the actual business, vendors, hosting region and applicable law before public launch.

Account and service data

We store your name, email, salted password hash, session-token hash, app names, hashed API keys, received logs, incident evidence, usage, alert settings and subscription status. Raw passwords and raw app keys are not stored. Logs are redacted for common bearer tokens, password/secret assignments and email addresses before storage. Redaction is best-effort; remove sensitive fields before sending logs.

Explanations and external providers

The explainer redacts common secrets, checks the built-in issue library and may send unmatched redacted log text to OpenAI when the operator configures that feature. RootCause requests non-persisted model responses; provider terms still apply. The free paste tool does not save the pasted text, but returns the explanation to your browser. Email, Slack and Telegram alerts are sent only when configured by the account owner.

Abuse prevention

To enforce the free explainer's daily cap, the service stores an HMAC-derived client IP value and the daily usage count in Neon, then deletes those counters after two days. The original IP is not stored in that table. Basic request rate limits also use the connection's client address in server memory.

Retention and deletion

Logs are removed according to the plan's retention setting (30 days Starter, 90 days Team). Deleting an app removes its logs, keys, incidents and notifications. Account deletion removes account-linked records. Backups, database-provider retention and external alert systems may have separate retention behavior controlled by the operator.

Security and payments

Sessions use HTTP-only, SameSite cookies. App keys and passwords are hashed. Slack and Telegram credentials are encrypted in Neon with a server-side encryption key. Razorpay processes subscription payments; RootCause stores subscription identifiers and signed webhook event records, not card details. Keep the server, database and encryption keys access-controlled.
